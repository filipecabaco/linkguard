One more step: let LinkGuard see websites

Your browser needs your OK before LinkGuard can check links. Press the button, then choose Allow.

LinkGuard is on.

It checks every link you see and stops the dangerous ones before they open. There's nothing you need to set up.

Not safe

Typed or opened a dangerous address? You get a full-page stop instead of the page.

Keep it in sight

Pin LinkGuard to your toolbar so you can see how many risky links a page has.

Smarter checks (optional)

Turn on an AI check called Jev for links that are new or tricky. It catches scams the built-in checks can miss.

When it's on, LinkGuard sends the link's address, its words and the name of the website you're on to Jev, made by TypeSafe. Nothing else from the page is sent, and it's only used to judge the link.

You pay TypeSafe directly for what you use. At their listed price, $1 covers tens of thousands of checks.

  1. Open the TypeSafe console

    It opens in a new tab. Sign in to your TypeSafe account. TypeSafe is in early access, so if you don't have an account yet, request one on their website and come back once you're in.

    Open TypeSafe console Request access

  2. Create a key and copy it

    On the API keys page, create a new key. Name it LinkGuard so you can recognise it later. Copy the whole key; it starts with apikey_.

  3. Paste it here

    LinkGuard tries one check straight away to make sure the key works. The key stays in this browser.

That's it. You can close this tab; LinkGuard keeps working.
