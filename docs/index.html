You might be wondering

What does it do?

It looks at every link on the pages you visit. Risky ones get marked: yellow means look twice, red means not safe. Click one and LinkGuard tells you why before anything opens.

If you type or open a dangerous address, you get a full-page stop instead of the site. You can always go back, or open it anyway.

Does it read my emails?

No. It only looks at the addresses of links, and it does that inside your browser. Nothing about the pages you visit is sent anywhere.

The one exception is optional: if you turn on smart checks, the links being checked are sent to an AI service. More on privacy.

Is it free?

Yes, and you don't need an account. Optional smart checks use your own TypeSafe account, which charges a tiny amount per check.

How do I install it?

Pick your browser. It takes about a minute.

  1. Download LinkGuard, then double-click the file to unzip it. You get a folder.
  2. Open chrome://extensions in a new tab
  3. Turn on Developer mode at the top right.
  4. Click Load unpacked and choose the folder. LinkGuard opens a welcome page when it's ready.

Download for Chrome

Chrome may remind you that the add-on came from outside its store. That's expected. New versions don't install themselves; check back here now and then.

What if it warns about a site I trust?

Press I trust this site on the warning and LinkGuard won't warn about it again. You can undo that in LinkGuard's settings.

Does it work on my phone?

Not yet. For now LinkGuard is for computers: Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera and Firefox.

What you'll see

LinkGuard stays out of the way until a link looks wrong. Then it shows up in one of three ways.

A yellow mark: look twice

Something about the link is odd, like a short link that hides where it goes. LinkGuard asks before opening it.

?bit.ly/free-gift-card

A red mark: not safe

The link is pretending to be somewhere it isn't. Clicking it shows you the real address and the reasons first.

!www.paypal.com

A full-page stop

If you type, paste or open a dangerous address, the site doesn't open. You get this instead, with one big button to go back.

What stays private

  • Links are checked on your computer.The built-in checks never send anything anywhere.
  • No account, no tracking, no ads.LinkGuard has no servers of its own and collects nothing about you.
  • Smart checks are off unless you turn them on.When on, only the link's address, its words and the website's name go to TypeSafe to judge the link.
